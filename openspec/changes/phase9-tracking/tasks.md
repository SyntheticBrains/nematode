### G.0 — the gap model's operating point (MUST, first in block G; gates G.1–G.3b)

**Cost**: ≈ 13–15 machine-hours, 3–4 engineering days for the gap-scale knob and the unit-weight, log-count and top-k wirings (roadmap Technical Debt 16; own change). **Why first**: in the settling loop a gap junction is additive excitatory drive, each pair's raw EM count divided by the square root of the two neurons' partner counts; on Cook 2019 ALA–CANL/R weigh 89.7 each, ALA's row sum is 232 against a median neuron's 2.6, 96 gap pairs sit above 1 — the weight at which a symmetric pair of tanh units becomes a self-sustaining latch — the heaviest latches sit on ALA, CAN and PVD off the sensory-to-motor path, a degree-preserving gap swap moves them onto it (about 14 pinned path neurons in the wild type against about 31 in a null draw), junctions touching ALA, CAN and PVD carry 38% of all effective coupling, and the gap scale has never been swept under PPO — a pin D16 forbids any contrast to run at. So "placement matters" may mean "where the six count outliers land". **Verdict map, fixed before any run**: `survives` — at least two-thirds of the anchor's effect remains under unit weights (c, 64 seeds) and the scattered outliers alone (f, 32 seeds) reproduce less than two-thirds of it, with the scale profile (e), gap-off (d) and depths (g) read as description beside; `artefact` — the lead falls below the minimum under unit weights, or the scattered outliers alone reproduce at least two-thirds of it; `unresolved` — otherwise. **`survives` licenses G.1–G.3b at the committed scale and depth, cited in every claim; `artefact` re-aims block G (G.0-A below); `unresolved` licenses nothing by itself** — how the block then proceeds is a dated decision here and in the roadmap. The misleading comment in `connectome_ppo.py` claiming the normalisation bounds per-neuron gap input is corrected with the knob. Every arm carries the D24 table and the shortcut audit.
